Clinical early warning requires an explicit balance between detecting deterioration and generating a manageable alert load. We propose to express that balance as a budget on the patient-average number of alarms per day and to calibrate a causal alarm policy with refractory and escalation state. The reference procedure aggregates dependent alarm sequences into patient-level losses and applies simultaneous finite-grid upper-confidence bounds under independent, identically distributed patient sampling, with the scorer, policy family and support bound fixed before calibration. A candidate is deployed only when its raw empirical loss plus a Hoeffding or empirical-Bernstein radius meets the budget; otherwise an exact no-alarm policy provides a safe fallback. The artificial-intelligence contribution is policy-level risk calibration that accounts for internal state, while the engineering contribution is an auditable operating point specified by an alert budget rather than an arbitrary threshold. The supplied implementation includes causal reference learners, official-format intensive-care data readers, and a synthetic effective-sample-size diagnostic. The proposed clinical protocol distinguishes population risk from finite-cohort exceedance, reports sensitivity alongside conditional detection lead time, and treats cross-hospital transfer without target calibration as distribution shift rather than guaranteed deployment. Real-cohort comparative performance and clinical effectiveness remain to be established.
