Clinical early warning must balance detection against a manageable alert load, yet threshold-independent discrimination metrics do not specify how often a deployed rule will alarm. We formulate early warning as a stateful alarm policy with a threshold, refractory window and escalation tier, and calibrate it against a patient-average budget on alarms per day. The artificial-intelligence contribution is a decision layer that converts any fixed risk score into a calibrated, auditable alarm policy: patient-level losses are combined with a finite-grid upper-confidence bound, a Learn-then-Test multiple-testing correction, and an optional randomised mixture of adjacent thresholds that can use a discrete grid's budget more fully. The engineering application is a causal sepsis-onset pipeline evaluated on 40{,}336 PhysioNet 2019 records from two hospitals, using prefix logistic, LightGBM and masked GRU scorers over three subject-disjoint seeds. On site A, mean sensitivity rises from 0.185 at 0.25 alarms per patient-day to 0.774 at 2 per day. Applying the source threshold to site B without target calibration lowers sensitivity at 1 per day from 0.585 to 0.315, even though the realised rate there is lower. Repeated-split coverage, label-free rate matching and a ward-load simulation quantify the price of the guarantee: the certificate is conservative at tight budgets and coarse grids, and target labels or local rate adaptation are needed to recover the operating point. The results support patient-blocked budget calibration as an auditable engineering instrument, but they do not establish clinical effectiveness, alert-fatigue reduction or deployment readiness.
