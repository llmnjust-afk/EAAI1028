\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper treated early warning as a stateful alarm policy whose alert load is a declared design constraint rather than an emergent threshold artefact. We derived a patient-blocked finite-grid upper-confidence procedure for the population mean of patient-level alarms per day, separated it from a deliberately invalid timestep effective-sample-size shortcut, and implemented the complete path from causal scoring through policy replay to auditable operating points. On the public 2019 A/B cohort, the procedure produced a monotone internal budget-sensitivity frontier, with A sensitivity rising from 0.185 at 0.25 alarms per patient-day to 0.774 at 2 per day over three seeds. The same A-calibrated threshold did not transport to site B: at the 1-per-day budget, B sensitivity was 0.315 versus 0.585 at A, even though the realised B alarm rate remained below the A certificate. The timestep comparator was less conservative at tight budgets and increased patient-level budget violations, while the GRU changed the internal frontier without removing the cross-site shift. These results support budgeted, patient-blocked calibration as an engineering instrument, but they do not establish clinical effectiveness, alert-fatigue reduction or deployment readiness; target-site recalibration, subgroup and missingness analyses, prospective outcome evaluation and a finer utility-aware grid remain necessary before any clinical claim.
