\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper treated early warning as a stateful alarm policy whose alert load is a declared design constraint. We derived a patient-blocked finite-grid upper-confidence procedure for the population mean of patient-level alarms per day, separated it from a deliberately invalid timestep shortcut, and implemented the path from causal scoring through policy replay to auditable operating points. On the 2019 A/B cohort, A sensitivity rose from 0.185 at 0.25 alarms per patient-day to 0.774 at 2 per day, but the same threshold did not transport: at 1 per day B sensitivity was 0.315 versus 0.585 at A, even though B's realised rate stayed below the request. Target recalibration raised B sensitivity to 0.497 at a rate of 0.616, and 10\% of eligible target subjects recovered the full-pool threshold at 1 per day. An independent 2012 mortality cohort confirmed that the budget remains portable while the transported operating point shifts, and the official 2019 utility showed that the best selector depends on whether the deployment values sensitivity or false-alarm cost. A validation-ranked CRC variant avoided over-alarming at the loose budget and raised official utility from 0.56 to 0.93 at 2 alarms per patient-day. These results support budgeted, patient-blocked calibration as an engineering instrument, not a clinical-effectiveness claim; prospective outcome evaluation and an independent same-task cohort remain necessary before deployment.
