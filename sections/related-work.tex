\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Backbones for Irregularly Sampled Clinical Time Series}

Recent work models irregular sampling explicitly, using continuous-time formulations \citep{shen2026enhancing}, reliability-aware temporal aggregation \citep{kim2026retamamba}, and graphs over observations and variables \citep{yalavarthi2024grafiti}. Hybrid sequence-graph designs report strong discrimination on the 2019 cohort \citep{khorram2026early}, while structure-preserving sequence models attempt to keep each alert connected to its supporting evidence \citep{ou2026sepsislens}. Complementary approaches model the observation process to synthesise irregular trajectories \citep{mehdizavareh2026informative} or exploit informative missingness for ward deterioration prediction \citep{mun2026made}. Domain-specific applications include venous thromboembolism detection after cancer treatment \citep{xu2026bridging}, while surveys synthesise the wider modelling landscape \citep{sun2026review}. These methods are potential future score backbones, not models already integrated or reproduced in the current package. Any integration must retain prefix-only information and cannot reuse a full-trajectory imputation procedure without a separate causality audit.

\subsection{Early Warning and Deterioration Prediction}

Clinical early warning has moved from static severity scores toward dynamic, trajectory-aware prediction. Classical early-warning scores remain important baselines and have been externally validated in emergency and prehospital settings \citep{candel2023development,goodacre2023prehospital}, but they are typically fixed-weight instruments rather than calibrated policies. Recent multicentre work predicts the shape of sepsis deterioration rather than a single risk estimate, arguing that heterogeneous trajectories require patient-specific forecasting \citep{xiumin2026machine}, while efficiency-oriented designs pursue compact models that remain explainable under the compute constraints of a ward workstation \citep{taparugssanagorn2026lightweight}. Others inject clinical reasoning into the prediction pathway: dynamic temporal graphs embed calculator-derived features so that the model inherits the semantics of established instruments \citep{yin2024sepsiscalc}, and language-model-guided simulation is used to generate interpretable physiological dynamics for early warning \citep{nie2026clinically}. Transfer-oriented formulations address the practical reality that a model trained in one institution must be reused in another with different coding practice and case mix \citep{komolafe2025early}, and earlier work established that deep models over longitudinal inpatient records can outperform tabular baselines for outcome prediction \citep{rong2025deep,yong2024deep,mohammad2024early}. Prospective implementation studies show that deployment changes the evidentiary question: a model that performs well offline may produce modest clinical effects and a substantial alert burden in practice \citep{adams2022prospective}. Multisite and external validations of sepsis models likewise report performance degradation and calibration drift across hospitals \citep{valan2025evaluating,ostermayer2024external}. Across this literature the model is usually the unit of contribution; the alerting rule that converts its output into an operational signal is treated as an afterthought, and comparison between methods is almost always conducted at a fixed threshold rather than at a fixed alarm volume.

\subsection{Calibration, Selective Prediction, and Conformal Guarantees}

Calibration and selective prediction have been studied in multimodal clinical classification \citep{lpez2026empirical}. Reliability estimation for time-series classifiers also examines evidence beyond output remapping \citep{cenacchi2026beyond}, while adaptation audits consider realistic label delays \citep{fentazi2026training}. Conformal risk control provides distribution-free control of an expected loss and has been extended to non-monotone losses and cross-validation variants \citep{angelopoulos2022conformal,angelopoulos2026conformal,cohen2024crossvalidation}. Time-series conformal methods handle temporal dependence explicitly \citep{angelopoulos2023conformal,english2024janet,lin2022conformal}, and prediction-set design has been studied for efficiency and human decision quality rather than coverage alone \citep{kiyani2024length,cresswell2024conformal}. Conformal methods have also been applied to physiological regression uncertainty \citep{strickland2025degrees}, to performance estimation under distribution shift \citep{lu2022estimating}, and to patient-specific haemodynamic adaptation \citep{li2026crosssample}. These contributions concern different units, targets and sampling assumptions; their guarantees cannot be declared invalid merely because an unrelated downstream alarm rule has memory. The present reference procedure instead specifies a patient-average alarm-rate estimand and uses independent-block concentration, without claiming the weaker assumptions or different risk statement of standard conformal constructions.

\subsection{Evaluation Practice}

Risk-of-bias instruments for prediction models address artificial-intelligence-specific issues in calibration, heterogeneity and applicability \citep{moons2025probastai}. Reviews of intensive-care adverse-event and time-series prediction also discuss reporting and evaluation limitations \citep{wang2026deep,hernandezdiaz2026deep,swinckels2024deep}. The alert-fatigue literature adds a complementary measurement problem: dismissal, override and workflow burden are not captured by discrimination or calibration \citep{ray2026alert,khan2026qualitative,jaidev2026sepsis}. Data-sharing histories and foundation-model agendas also shape which cohorts are available for replication \citep{clifford2026from}, and task heterogeneity across diagnoses complicates direct transfer of mortality models \citep{xu2025diagnosisbased,papareddy2025transforming}. These concerns motivate a protocol that reports requested budgets, realised alarm rates, sensitivity and lead time alongside discrimination. The present evaluation follows that protocol on the public 2019 cohort; shared requested budgets make the operational constraint explicit but do not imply identical realised alert volume across models.
